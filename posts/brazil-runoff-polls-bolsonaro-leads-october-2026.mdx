The first poll since Brazil's October 4 first round put Flávio Bolsonaro on 53% against Lula's 47% in the runoff. PoderData surveyed 3,000 voters across 705 municipalities from October 5 to 7. The IPESPE poll released Saturday October 10 put Bolsonaro at 52.7%.

Prediction markets are less cautious. As of October 11, Polymarket gives Bolsonaro an 88% chance of winning and Kalshi has him at 87%. Around $180 million has been traded on the Polymarket election market.

The problem is that the same polls had Lula ahead before October 4. Most final surveys showed Lula winning the first round. He didn't. Bolsonaro beat him by 1.87 percentage points. The miss prompted Brazilian deputy Nikolas Ferreira to call for a parliamentary inquiry into the polling firms the Monday after the vote.

## The polling miss is a market issue now

Brazilian assets have been pricing a Bolsonaro win since before the first round. The Ibovespa rose 2.63% on October 2 and gained about 5% through election week. The logic is straightforward: Bolsonaro has committed to a primary surplus target and market-friendly advisers. Petrobras dividend policy under his government is expected to be more generous than under Lula.

A 5 to 6 point Bolsonaro lead in the polls looks comfortable. The same kind of lead looked comfortable on October 3. The margin of error on most runoff surveys is around 1.8 percentage points. If five of nine national polls had Lula ahead before a vote where he lost by 1.87 points, the current surveys may be covering a much narrower real gap than they appear to.

## The two scenarios

A Bolsonaro win on October 25 is largely in the price. Modest relief rally on the Ibovespa, the real firms, Petrobras holds its dividend outlook. The more interesting question is what he can actually deliver, since he needs a working congressional coalition and the legislative map shapes that more than the presidential result does.

A Lula win would be a genuine shock given current polling and prediction markets. Petrobras is the immediate focal point. His history of pressuring the company on domestic fuel pricing is documented and well-feared by the market. The real weakens. Emerging market funds with Brazil exposure face redemption pressure fast.

The Ibovespa is sitting about 5% above where it was before the first round. October 25 will tell us whether that premium was deserved.
