Gold fell 0.54% to $4,171 on Monday. The US 10-year yield held near 5.24%. Brent crude was around $91.50. Gold is sitting below all three of its main moving averages.

At $4,171, gold is still historically elevated. Six months ago this price would have seemed extraordinary. The issue isn't absolute levels. It's that the thing driving gold's 2026 rally, the energy supply shock and its inflation effects, is showing early signs of easing. Brent has come off from its September highs above $107. Hormuz flows have partially recovered. The diesel supply shortfall remains, but the worst-case scenario for global energy markets in 2026 has not played out.

## September CPI on Wednesday

The Federal Reserve meets on October 28. September CPI, due Wednesday October 14, is the last inflation print before that decision. Analysts are watching for a reading above 3.6% year on year. A hot print pushes the dollar and yields higher, adds more pressure to gold in the near term. A softer read below 3.4% relieves some of the yield pressure and likely gives bullion a bounce.

Oil's pullback from September highs means the September print may not capture the full peak energy cost impact. That may show up more in the October data. If that's right, a softer September number could be misleading about the underlying trajectory, and gold's current weakness may be a false signal rather than a genuine turning point.

## The yield side is winning

Gold's historic role is as a hedge against monetary instability and inflation. Both of those things are still present in 2026. But the bond market is pricing a Fed that stays restrictive: higher short rates, more Treasury supply, quantitative tightening reducing the biggest buyer of long-dated debt. That combination pushes real yields up and non-yielding assets down.

The longer-term picture for gold isn't bearish. Central bank buying has been strong all year. De-dollarisation themes are active. Any diplomatic resolution on Iran that crashes energy prices would likely hit equities hard and send safe haven flows straight into gold. But at $4,171 with the 10-year at 5.24%, the near-term direction depends on what lands in the CPI print on Wednesday morning.
